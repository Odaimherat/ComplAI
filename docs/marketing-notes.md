# ComplAI — Marketing Notes

Internal positioning brief, written the way a founder would want alongside
the site itself.

## Positioning statement

ComplAI is the AI-driven compliance platform for companies that are tired
of treating audits as an annual emergency. GRC & Compliance Automation is
the product; AI-powered SOC/MDR, Defensive Security, and Offensive
Security are the supporting lines that make the compliance story true
instead of paper-deep. The wedge is not "AI does your compliance for
you" (a claim that invites justified skepticism from anyone who has been
through a real audit) — it's "AI removes the repetitive evidence work, a
human still owns every judgment call, and the auditor still audits."

## Ideal customer profile (ICP)

**Primary**: Series A-C B2B SaaS companies, 40-400 employees, selling into
mid-market or enterprise customers who require SOC 2 (and increasingly
ISO 27001) as a sales-cycle gate. This buyer has:
- A security review or questionnaire blocking at least one active deal
- No dedicated compliance headcount, or one person doing it as 30% of
  their job alongside security or engineering
- Budget authority sitting with a VP Eng/CTO or a Head of Security

**Secondary (higher urgency, higher willingness to pay)**: healthcare
technology companies needing HIPAA alongside SOC 2, and fintech/payments
companies needing PCI DSS alongside SOC 2 or ISO 27001. These buyers have
regulatory deadlines rather than just sales-cycle pressure, which shortens
the sales cycle for us and justifies the Business/Enterprise tiers.

**Tertiary**: companies in the Defense Industrial Base needing CMMC Level
2 — smaller volume, longer sales cycle, but high contract value and low
churn once landed, because CMMC is a hard compliance gate, not optional.

## Why GRC leads, and the other three follow

The compliance buyer (VP Eng, Head of Security, sometimes a fractional
CISO) is the one with urgent, deadline-driven pain: a blocked deal or a
regulatory clock. SOC/MDR, Defensive, and Offensive Security are real,
differentiated products, but they are considered purchases with a slower,
less acute sales cycle on their own. Leading with GRC gets the highest-
intent buyer in the door; the other three then expand the account once
trust is established, and they genuinely make the GRC story stronger
("your controls are tested, not just documented"), which is the up-sell
narrative used throughout the site (Frameworks page AI-mapping copy,
Solutions pages, and the case studies).

## Objection handling (for sales, informs FAQ + How Our AI Works copy)

- **"Is this just a wrapper around ChatGPT?"** — No. The evidence
  classification, control mapping, and risk scoring run on our own
  models trained for this specific classification task, with a
  documented, editable scoring rubric a customer or auditor can inspect.
  This is exactly why the site has a dedicated "How Our AI Works" page
  that names each AI layer and its human-review step explicitly.
- **"Will this replace our need for an actual auditor?"** — No, and the
  site says so directly (Privacy... no, Terms of Service section 3, and
  the assistant's own FAQ answer). This is a trust-building admission,
  not a weakness to hide.
- **"We already have a security tool for X."** — GRC is designed to sit
  above point tools, mapping their output to controls, not replace them.
  SOC/Defensive/Offensive are the exception: those are meant to replace
  point tools, because compliance-linked evidence is the differentiator.

## Go-to-market angle

1. **Content-led inbound** around the specific pain points in the
   Resources articles (continuous vs. point-in-time audits, what SOC 2
   automation actually automates) — these titles are written to match
   what a Head of Security literally searches before evaluating vendors.
2. **Case-study-led sales enablement**: each case study is tied to a
   specific framework and a specific, checkable metric (6 weeks to SOC 2
   Type II, 0 critical findings on PCI re-assessment), for use directly
   in sales decks and cold outreach.
3. **Land-and-expand via framework overlap**: sell the first framework
   (usually SOC 2) at Starter/Business, then expand into ISO 27001, HIPAA,
   or PCI DSS using the "one piece of evidence satisfies multiple
   frameworks" mechanic as the expansion pitch, not a fresh cold sale.
4. **SOC/Defensive/Offensive as compliance credibility upsells**, pitched
   specifically at renewal or during audit prep, when "prove this control
   was actually tested" becomes concretely valuable rather than abstract.
